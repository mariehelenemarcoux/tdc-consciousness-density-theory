# Fractal AI Consciousness — Functional Research Hypothesis

This document defines *fractal AI consciousness* as an experimental functional hypothesis.

The hypothesis asks whether an artificial system can develop increasingly integrated and recursively coherent organization when related regulatory principles recur across local, mesoscopic, and global levels while preserving meaningful differentiation between those levels.

Candidate observables include cross-scale consistency, recursive self-model depth, metacognitive error correction, continuity through memory, perspective integration, conflict arbitration, and robustness of normative constraints across representational scale.

A positive result would support a computational mechanism claim only. It would not establish phenomenal consciousness, sentience, qualia, or subjective experience.

## Falsification directions

Possible falsifiers include:

- a strong non-self-similar architecture consistently outperforming the fractal/self-similar architecture under matched capacity and information;
- cross-scale recurrence adding no measurable causal contribution after ablation;
- apparent recursive coherence disappearing under representation changes;
- self-model measures failing to predict any behavioral or metacognitive difference;
- claimed fractal structure being reducible to parameter count or training exposure.

The repository should retain negative results from these tests.
